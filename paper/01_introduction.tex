\section{Introduction}

Serving infrastructure for large language models is reorganizing into
separated planes: a data plane that executes inference, a control plane
that governs admission, routing, and placement, and a management layer for
configuration and observation. The control planes shipping today provide
gateways, rate limits, per-request and per-session budget caps, priority
scheduling with preemption, and account-level reserved throughput. A
growing class of workloads arrives beneath this layer in a shape it was
not built for: long-horizon agentic flows, units of work with stable
identity across many model invocations, accumulating state whose loss
destroys progress, idling between invocations while tools run, and
delivering value only at completion. This paper specifies a serving
discipline built for such flows, a completion-oriented resource contract
granted at admission, and then does to it what proposals are rarely made
to endure: evaluates it under a specification frozen before the
evaluation system existed, with published thresholds under which the
proposal's central claim dies.

It dies. The discipline's economic claim, that per-flow completion
guarantees buy more completed work per provisioned GPU-hour than they
strand, loses a median 10.3 percent to the best of six equally tuned
baselines across a 108-cell pre-registered grid, supporting the
hypothesis in 0 of 108 cells; a second hypothesis, that degradation keyed
to committed capacity degrades more gracefully under overload than
deterministic demotion, is likewise killed, reversing sign when a
measurement artifact was corrected. The cost is structural: an agentic
flow idles in tool time at roughly a 5 percent duty cycle, so capacity
reserved for it is stranded at least 88 percent regardless of configuration,
and the deficit peaks exactly at nominal provisioning, where a
well-provisioned fleet's work-conserving schedulers are at their best.

The mechanism makes two kinds of claims, and the verdict divides them.
The economic claims are tested and killed, and this paper reports the
kill with its full history, including the amendments and integrity
interventions that cut in both directions on the way to it. The
enforcement and architectural claims, that a reservation-holding control
plane gives an organization admission where policy binds, a budget that
contains any runaway to a number fixed in advance, an audit ledger, and a
kill authority that does not route through the model being doubted, were
never throughput claims; two of them are measured here in the mechanism's
favor, and the rest hold by construction. What the evaluation removes is
the ability to sell those properties as free; what it establishes is
their price.

The evaluation's yield exceeds its verdict, and the findings survive it.
Under adversarial runaway injection, the discipline completes half of all
compliant agentic flows with a bounded three-point degradation, while the
two strongest request-oriented baselines complete none: instrumented
admission starvation, one of 107 compliant flows ever scheduled, with
zero preemptions, the priority-versus-contract distinction converted from
argument to table. Reservations improve interactive tail latency by 63 to 97 percent across most of the region, isolation priced in stranding, with
one measured corner where it inverts. The sign of the primary comparison
flips in the mechanism's favor past saturation, locating the regime
boundary where guarantees begin to pay. And the evaluation itself
produced a measurement lesson for a benchmark category now multiplying:
horizons shorter than the workload's flow lifetimes silently corrupt both
completion metrics and baseline tuning, and we give the quantified bias
and the structural fix.

This paper makes five contributions. First, a complete serving-layer
discipline for long-horizon agentic flows, specified to implementation
depth: jointly guaranteed reservations over compute, a cumulative
cross-invocation token budget, and pinned cache; a hard non-preemption
guarantee; a bounded reserved subset over aggregate classes; degradation
keyed to committed headroom; a management plane with authority and
availability independent of the inference stack; and trust-keyed
admission, each element the deliberate transfer of a lesson packet
networking paid for (Section 3). Second, the first controlled comparison
of completion-oriented versus preemption-oriented scheduling for such
workloads: seven policies on identical simulated hardware with
byte-identical workload realizations per cell, under an equal-budget
tuning protocol enforced by test. Third, the quantified decomposition of
what completion guarantees cost and what they buy: stranding, isolation,
containment, and the load boundary, each measured. Fourth, the measured
starvation result for request-oriented scheduling under agentic load.
Fifth, the horizon-censoring finding with its structural fix. The
pre-registration machinery, frozen thresholds, a numbered amendment log,
an input-domain guard on the verdict path, and the release of every
artifact including superseded readouts, is described as method, because
for a paper whose headline is its own proposal's death, the method is the
product.
